Methods & additional information — full-record traces

The envelope. The shaded band is a min–max envelope per 6-minute bin: all raw samples inside each bin (the photometry stream is ~15-s resolution) are reduced to that bin's minimum and maximum, and the band spans them. Nothing is averaged in this reduction — the band shows the full range of values the signal took within the bin, so volatility and motion artifacts remain visible instead of being smoothed away. The 10 trace samples per pixel on screen are what make high-frequency structure inspectable without shipping raw data.

Correction. Signal = 470 nm (sensor excitation) divided by 415 nm (isosbestic control) — the lab's divide-by-isosbestic convention — then a 30-s centered median filter, then ΔF/F₀ with F₀ = mean of ZT14–22 on cycle 1. A third layer (raw corrected signal) can be toggled on with the chip above the chart.

The gold line. Light-pulse on/off, drawn on the canvas (not as a data series) so it never distorts the y-axis. Pulse timing comes from the photodiode log or lab params, never guessed.

Sections 01–03 trace one session at a time — one cage of animals recorded together. The Session picker changes the cage; the Animal picker changes the fiber within it. The two sessions are separate experiments (Sep 27 – Oct 14 and Nov 14–28, 2022), not two views of the same animals — except where section 06 flags a re-cut. Zeitgeber time is cumulative from recording start (ZT0 = lights-on convention of the prior LD cycle; in DD it free-runs). ΔF/F₀ baseline = mean of ZT14–22 on cycle 1, per the lab's MATLAB pipeline.

02Actogram

The classic circadian biology view: each row is one day, hours left→right. Darker = more wheel running. In constant darkness a healthy clock free-runs with τ slightly under 24 h, so activity onsets drift left. After the light pulse (gold row marker) the rhythm shifts — that's phase resetting, the SCN doing its job.

— 10-min bins · wheel revolutions

Methods & additional information — actogram

Construction. Each row = one day of the recording, hours left→right (ZT, cumulative from recording start). Wheel revolutions are binned at 10 min; intensity is bin count scaled to the 98th percentile (so one hyperactive night doesn't wash out the rest). Rows are absolute recording days — no double-plotting — with the light-pulse day banded in gold and the prior-LD subjective day left unshaded.

Reading it. In constant darkness the rhythm free-runs with period τ (this cohort: 23.0–24.5 h, χ² periodogram on 10-min bins, 20–28 h window). τ < 24 h drifts onsets leftward day over day; a phase shift after the pulse appears as an abrupt horizontal jump that persists. Compare with section 04's zoom for what the pulse does to the calcium trace itself.

Methods & additional information — cohort's day

Onset detection. Activity onsets from the validated detector used across this dashboard: wheel events histogrammed at 30-min resolution, convolved with a 6-h window, each day's minimum of the smoothed profile marking rest onset; activity onset = minimum + 6 h. Onsets unwrapped to a monotone series; cycles outside 18–30 h are dropped as detection failures.

CT normalization. Each consecutive onset pair defines one cycle. Within a cycle, time is rescaled linearly so the interval maps onto CT12–36 (exactly 24 circadian hours); ΔF/F and wheel rate are linearly interpolated onto the common 96-bin CT grid (15 CT-min per bin). The pulse cycle and first post-pulse cycle are excluded, so transient phase-jumping never enters the average. Per-animal mean across its cycles, then group mean ± SEM across animals.

Known limits. (1) The wheel stream is the anchor, so the behavior profile is partly self-fulfilling — the calcium trace is the read-out of interest. (2) Linear stretching assumes phase evolves uniformly within a cycle; it can't represent within-cycle waveform compression differences. (3) A calcium-anchored alternative (daily ΔF/F trough = CT18) exists in the pipeline and can be built on request. (4) τ values: 6577 = 23.0 h, 6976 = 24.5, 6977 = 24.0, 6978 = 24.5, 7390 = 24.0 (median inter-onset intervals).

Raw-time panel. ZT-locked 30-min bins of ΔF/F and wheel rate per animal, group-averaged. Shown because straight averaging was requested as a first pass; the smearing from τ ≠ 24 is exactly why the CT-normalized panels exist.

04The light pulse

Zoomed to ±12 h around the pulse (15-second resolution). This is photic phase resetting in action: a 30-min light pulse in the behavioral night acutely suppresses then releases SCN calcium, and the clock's phase is rewritten for the days that follow.

— light pulse

Pre/post periods below come from a χ² periodogram (Sokolove–Bushell) on wheel activity before vs. after the pulse. In DD, mice run ~23.3–23.6 h cycles — shorter than 24 h, as expected for the species.

Methods & additional information — the light pulse

Window. ±12 h around pulse onset at the full 15-s resolution (the only section where raw-resolution traces are shown), so the acute response — suppression during the pulse, release after — is separable from the circadian-scale rhythm. Envelope as in section 01: per-bin min–max, nothing averaged.

Pre/post periods. χ² periodogram (Sokolove–Bushell 1978) on 10-min wheel histograms, 20–28 h window, computed separately on pre-pulse and post-pulse days. The lab's MATLAB pipeline computes these identically; the Python port matches it to r = 1.0000 on the pilot (methods section).

What to look for. The photic response is biphasic in calcium: acute suppression while the light is on, then rebound. The phase consequence is not in this panel — it's sections 05–06 (shift in each clock, and when the pulse landed) and 07 (recovery of the shift windows).

05Two clocks, one pulse

Each animal carries two measurable oscillators: when it starts running (the behavioral clock — wheel onsets) and when SCN calcium reaches its daily minimum (the calcium clock). The light pulse rewrites both — but not identically, and the disagreements are the interesting part.

Methods & additional information — LP in CT

CT of the pulse. In each clock's frame, the pre-pulse onset (or trough) regression is extrapolated to the pulse; the fractional cycle position converts to CT. Behavioral frame: wheel onsets = CT12. Calcium frame: daily ΔF/F minima = CT18 (night trough). The same physical pulse lands at different CT in the two frames — that discrepancy is itself the measurement of how far the clocks have drifted apart.

Shifts. Same estimator as section 05: pre/post linear fit difference at the pulse cycle, ×24/τ_pre to circadian hours. One pulse per animal means no within-animal dose-response; five points describe where this cohort sat on the curve, not the curve itself.

Methods & additional information — repeat recordings

The envelope, exactly. Every span's shaded band is the per-bin min–max of the ΔF/F samples inside each 6-minute bin — the band spans every value the signal took within the bin, nothing is averaged, so motion spikes and dropouts stay visible at a glance. (For the pilot recording's own spans the same convention holds at 15-s resolution in the pulse window, section 04.)

dF/F for these spans. Era-B mats (the newer file format, June 2022 onward) carry flat/unprocessed signal variables; ΔF/F is recomputed with the lab convention (signal/415, 30-s median filter, F₀ = ZT14–22 mean of cycle 1) — the same correction the validated pipeline applies, re-run on the raw channels the mat still carries.

Behavior streams. Wheel = revolutions rebuilt from raw CSVs with the bit-exact loader (validated corr 1.0 against the lab's ground truth). Motion = total-cage motion, a different modality — dashed, labeled, never mixed with wheel, and no onset analysis is claimed on it. "No behavior stream" = the recording carries neither.

Light pulses. Only photodiode-verified or lab-params pulses are marked. Photodiode logs in the motion era lack day-of-year, so pulse times can't always be anchored to the timeline — "no verified light pulse" means exactly that, not "no pulse occurred."

Methods & additional information — quality control

Two independent screens. (1) Circadian SNR: χ² periodogram of ΔF/F (not wheel), amplitude at peak period divided by mean background — a rhythm detector. (2) Sine amplitude: 24-h sinusoid least-squares fit to the full ΔF/F record, amplitude z-scored against the record's own noise — a waveform-strength detector. An animal needed to fail both to be excluded by the model; the researcher who collected the data then reviewed every call (6577 kept on rhythm clarity despite borderline SNR; 7381's exclusion is consistent with its fiber position being off-target).

Gallery windows. 48 h starting 24 h into each recording, 6-min min–max envelope, wheel (or motion where wheel is absent) on the right axis where available — same construction as section 01 so failed and kept traces are directly comparable.

09Running and the clock

Three questions asked of the wheel data: does a heavy night buy a quieter following day (the mice could be sleeping more soundly)? Does more running change the next cycle's length? And do the light shifts at recording start contaminate the first days?

Night revolutions vs next subjective day —

Per circadian cycle, behavioral clock. No compensation: heavy nights do not buy detectably quieter days in this cohort.

The rate-normalization artifact r with next cycle length

Dividing revolutions by window length suggests a strong negative activity→period effect (pooled r = −0.41, p = 0.001) — but the window is the outcome variable. Permuting counts while keeping windows (structural null) reproduces most of it. Honest verdict: no per-cycle activity feedback detectable here.

Phase shift vs leading-days exclusion behavioral clock

Dropping the first 1–3 days (light-shift transient at recording start) does not shrink the two large advances — if anything it grows them. But the pre-pulse onset count shrinks with each skip (7390: 6 → 3), so the growing values increasingly rest on thin fits. The sign of every animal's conclusion is stable; magnitudes beyond skip-1 are fragile.

Methods & additional information — running and the clock

Compensation. Per circadian cycle (behavioral clock): total night revolutions vs total day revolutions, pooled r across cycles with a permutation p. The question is behavioral (does a heavy night buy a quieter day?), the answer is no detectable compensation in this cohort.

Rate-normalization artifact. Dividing revolutions by window length correlates the predictor with the outcome by construction; the structural null (permuting counts, keeping windows) reproduces most of the raw r = −0.41. This panel exists to show why the rate version is uninterpretable, not to report it as a finding.

Leading-days exclusion. Shift re-estimated with the first 1–3 recording days dropped, to test sensitivity to the LD→DD release transient. Onset counts per fit are shown because the stability of the estimate tracks them.

How the data was processed

Acquisition. Neurophotometrics 3-color fiber photometry: 470 nm (GCaMP/GFlamp1/GFP excitation), 415 nm (isosbestic control), 560 nm (tdTomato reference). LED states are frame-multiplexed in a single raw CSV stream; channels are demultiplexed on the 0.0039 trigger edge as in the lab's ProcessPhotometryAndWheelData.m.

Correction. Signal = 470/415 (divide-by-isosbestic for all animals here), 30-s centered median filter, ΔF/F₀ with F₀ = mean over ZT 14–22 of the first cycle. Wheel events (ZT-hour timestamps of revolutions) are histogrammed into 10-min bins.

Port validation. The Python demux was compared sample-for-sample against the MATLAB FullTrace.mat outputs: raw channels matched to ~1e-16, corrected signal r = 0.9998, ΔF/F r = 1.0000 (max abs diff ≈ 1.4e-6) on the pilot animal; the cross-session check runs live below against a second-session animal.

Circadian metrics. χ² periodogram (Sokolove & Bushell 1978) on 10-min wheel histograms, 20–28 h period window, computed separately pre/post light pulse.

What this is and isn't

This is a data exploration dashboard over two exemplar sessions (2022-10-14, n=4; 2022-11-28, n=6) pulled from a 1.5-year, 1.23 TB archive — not a full-cohort study. Trace sections are descriptive; the analysis sections (05–10) report small-sample statistics with permutation tests and explicit fragility flags — n is 5 animals, so treat every correlation as a lead, not a conclusion. Session 221014 contains VIP-Cre and D1-Cre animals (recording quality permitting); 221128 is D1-Cre only, with sensor-type contrasts (GCaMP6s / GFlamp1 / GFP control).

Raw recordings and the full histology set remain on the lab's Dropbox; this page ships only downsampled derived traces (~2 MB) plus one compressed histology section per animal (~90 KB each, ~2.4 MB).
